## Introduction

World of Goo 2 is a sequel to the 2008 game with the same name, it is a game solely about putting balls into a hole. Let me rephrase, it's a game where you create a structure to reach a pipe at the end that will suck your balls. Ok, that's not better... Let's try again, you have balls, pipes, sucking and fluids and you... Ok, jokes aside. The game is created by the same studio 2D Boy and now with the help of Tomorrow Corporation, which is basically another studio with the same indie developers as the first one, or almost the same. You can even see the same art style from the World of Goo series in the Tomorrow Corporation games that include great titles like Human Resource Machine and 7 Billion Humans that are both also puzzle games but less physics and more programming-like puzzles.

## Gameplay

In this game, like I was jokingly saying before, you have balls, Goo Balls, I think they are called, which you can connect like joints to form some kind of semi-rigid structure. If you played something like Poly Bridge you get the idea, but unlike the bridge builder game, this has more variety in directions of construction and types of joints aka Goo Balls. Your objective is to create a structure that somehow reaches the end pipe with a number of spare Goo Balls. Easy enough, right? But the game, as it progresses, like in the previous installment, it throws different balls at you. One that burns, another that is detachable, another that allows only 1-joint structures and not the usual minimum of two or more. A lot of these types were already present in the first game, with the most notable new addition being the fluid mechanics that are present in the majority of the game somehow. Sometimes as a friend, sometimes as an enemy that you must divert or avoid. In that sense, the game brings enough innovation to be a different game and even more with things that I will not spoil, but which surprised me in Chapter 4 of the game.

## Difficulty & Replayability

The game still has its difficulty, although some of the fluid levels I think tone the difficulty down by much, making it more of a tedious task than an enjoyable one. These games, although the first one I played when I was very young, made me think about how I would structure things, where I had to reinforce the structure and so on, and that challenge was fun, and for those levels and some levels in Chapter 4 which have a fun gimmick that strays a little, although the latter ones were entertaining and a welcome change of pace. Lastly, about this, I think some of the levels have too much similarity to the first one, not enough to be mad about it, but enough for me to notice it. Take it however you want. Ah, before I forget. All the chapters have optional hard levels, which require some thought and all levels have challenges that require a maximum number of moves, clearing it in less than X time and saving X balls (more than the minimum required to pass the level) which can increase the duration of the game for a completionist.

## Soundtrack

One thing that surprised me in the game was the OST, although it does not have any masterpiece or ultra-memorable songs but are catchy enough for me to be humming along while in the level from funny songs, to ominous songs, to epic songs, a mix of everything that goes well with the crazy vague story of this game and all the Goo Ball shenanigans. Have you noticed they sound like minions?

## Story

Before I go to the last part, I should talk a little about the story, I guess? It's the same anti-corporation capitalism satire as ever, with stupid jokes and caricatured characters as ever. So it doesn't have much depth, but sometimes it can amuse you.

## Verdict

Lastly, the game at full price is $29 which can be a little expensive for 5 hours of game if you do not want to do all the levels, with all the challenges and even hang around more with community-made levels. So if you like some fun medium physics-based puzzles and like to challenge yourself with the extra objectives I recommend it to you, if not, probably wait for a discount. Do not get me wrong, Price/hours ratio is a shitty metric, but it's good to be aware.
