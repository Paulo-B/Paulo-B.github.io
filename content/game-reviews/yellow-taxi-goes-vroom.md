I do not know when this game went to my wishlist. I think it was somewhere in the time I watched more Twitch, and I saw someone play it, only for a few minutes, and I had to add it to my wishlist and close the stream. I knew I did not want to know more about it. But for a moment, let's imagine that I am a different person, someone who found this in another way. Imagine that you are browsing Steam, looking for something to play. Something new in some sense. You do not know in which sense, but you keep looking. And, eventually, you find this weird title, a title that makes you open your mouth in a slight grin, a smile or even a chuckle. "Yellow Taxi Goes Vroom" is the name of the game. You ponder for a bit: is it a racing game? Is it a different take on Crazy Taxi, or some kind of lower-budget spiritual successor? You read the tags and, to your surprise: 3D PLATFORMER. Wait, what? What do you mean? With a Taxi? That sounds silly. Before buying the game, you, for curiosity, look up who made it. No one you know especially well: Panik Arcade, who also developed CloverPit (a rogue-lite with graphics that remind you of Buckshot Roulette). You find that the studio is Italian and was founded by two people. Okay, never mind that, the game got your curiosity. The price: 16,50€. Not expensive. You buy it.

The game starts and you are presented with your co-protagonist, Morio. A character that looks like some kind of mechanic, maybe pipe mechanic, Italian pipe mechanic, I think I am missing a word. And you are his latest creation: a taxi, specifically, a wind-up toy car, and you have to save the world from Alien Mosk, CEO of Tosla, that is polluting the world with their new green gears oil energy. The stupidity of the story makes you want to keep going.

Controller in hand, you start guiding your character, your car. Well, they are both the same. Initially it is clunky, you do not understand very well how the controls work. They seem like some kind of tank controls. It reminds you of old games like Resident Evil (the older ones). The moment of confusion and of testing lets you appreciate the graphics and the background music. Although more modern, it makes you remember the graphics from Super Mario 64 or Gex: Enter the Gecko. After the initial hurdle, you find yourself in the hub (one of them). You enter the first level. You unlock your first, only and most important ability: the Flip-o-Will. Basically a boost to your car. And that lets you overcome the first hurdle. Then you discover you can cancel the Flip-o-Will to jump, and that if you press it at the right time, you get more speed. The first gear is collected. Wait... this level has 5? Wait, what are those bunnies? Hey, I found a cute hat for my taxi. Oh no, I am hooked.

Leaving this "hypothetical" story behind, you should already understand the gist of the game. A collectathon where you have to get a lot of gears; it's impossible to look at this 3D platformer collectathon and not think of Mario Odyssey. It smells of its influence; I think the only thing missing is the funny names for each moon, in this case, gear. But this game already has a lot of humor going through it. The taxi itself, Tosla, Alien Mosk, the stupid backgrounds, the weird characters that you find and talk to throughout it. We cannot forget the character (recurrent) that tells you that any similarity with real people is coincidental and that is a game of pure fiction. Or the Chads who say that they only train legs once a month, and I will not spoil any more of it. Or even the stupid (and they get stupider) hats that you find for your taxi. Why the hell would a taxi need a hat? The thing where I feel Mario's influence the most is the mechanics of the platforming. They are easy to grasp, maybe at the beginning a bit clunky. But once you get the feel, it starts to feel good. I was using the car as a car in the first levels, but then something clicked for me and I started using it like a platformer. And the freedom that that gives makes me wonder whether I got some of the gears the intended way. More than once, I think not. I put the car at an angle, did a super Flip-o-Will and the car started flying. I got to some places where I am not sure I was supposed to be, but, like in Mario, there was gold there, so they did know. It has that same magic of trying to be faster, better and look and find your next thing. The level is your oyster, or however the saying goes. However, some parts are clunky in the platforming even after some hours with it. Sometimes you do not get the speed you wish because you did not hit the ramp at the right angle, for example. The game knows this and is made like that so you abuse it, but still... Sometimes a bit more consistency would be better.

The levels are diverse, so you have different feels. And even different type of platforming or gameplay. For example, in one level (more than one in reality), Morio is not there. So you have to ask people to wind up your character/Taxi, and so you have an interesting and annoying mechanic: a timer. To get more time, you have to deliver people Crazy Taxi style while you look for the gears, or collect the clock pickups spread around the level (alongside the coins), which raise your timer by an amount that depends on their type. Just so you know, the only use of the coins is to buy more hats, so do not worry. Going back to the levels. There are levels like the wind-up ones, there are levels that are more fast-paced, more vertical, more precise platforming, a bit of everything. And, although I would have liked the difficulty turned up a bit, there are extra levels that you have to unlock after beating the main story for the first time that are based on the original ones (at least thematically) and have a greater challenge, though not as much as I wanted. In all the levels that had it, sometimes the timer was a bit annoying when you want to look up for the missing gears, and there is one particular level that has something that one-shots you that I wouldn't touch with a 10-foot pole.

Do I have anything more to tell you? Not much. The OST is nice but not memorable. What makes this game great is the same thing that made Odyssey great. Learning that those simple mechanics can be made better, more perfect, and you go faster and farther with each jump. And finding, looking, searching to find the secrets of the levels. I 100%'d (achievement-wise) the game on Steam, collected all except the hats, beat every level and I could not be happier. I wish there were more to play, more memes, more floating mustaches in the background of the level. I just wanted, like a child, to keep doing vroom vroom with my car.
