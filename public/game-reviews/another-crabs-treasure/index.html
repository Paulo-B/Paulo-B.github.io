Another Crab's Treasure

Sep 09, 2026

8/10

Personal rating

A crab’s life should be easy: you go into your shell, protect yourself from the birds and live your life. There on your shore, without anything to worry about. What the hell is a job? What the hell are taxes? What even is the massive construct of society that oppresses us, that makes us always be productive in the name of money, which we call Capitalism? That’s what Kril, the protagonist of this game, would tell you before starting their story. This is the game created by Aggro Crab studio, a small indie studio located in Seattle, which a lot of you may know because of another game they developed afterwards, called PEAK. It follows the story of a hermit crab that sees their life change from one moment to the next, because of unpaid taxes, taxes that started some tides ago because some duchess said so. But Kril, not familiarized with the concept of taxes, has no money (trash) to pay these taxes, and the IRS (Shark Stick? I do not know what to call it) embargoes Kril’s shell. From that moment on, their mission is only one: recover their shell. As you, my attentive reader, may have noticed, the currency in this world is trash, more specifically, for us players, microplastics. The sea, polluted by humans, and the sea-life (crabs, hermits and others) transform the economy to be dependent on trash: you have to work for trash, you have to have trash; if you have no trash you belong to the low layers of society, if you have a lot, you are an elite. But this has consequences, as we know, trash and pollution are dangerous for us (humans) but also for sea life like the ones that belong to this story. And that produces gunk, which transforms the citizens of the sea into brainwashed, crazy, murderous beings.

At this moment, you ask me: what genre would this game be? Platformer? Adventure? Walking Simulator? Nope, none of that. A soulslike. Are you surprised? Because I was. Well, going back to the story. A little after starting the search for the shark that stole Kril’s shell, they find a fork, which will be their weapon to protect themselves from the crabs with black eyes. And at that moment we feel the first touches of what the combat looks like. There is no stamina, so no typical soulslike action handling, but there is the dodge, the i-frames, the physical pain on yourself when you get hit because you almost die in one hit, and the fear of going back to the checkpoints, which are the Moon Snail Shells that act like the typical bonfires, and having to retrace all your steps. Additionally, although a little after getting the fork, you learn that you can change the shell, and this brings a curious but slight change in the typical mechanics of shielding and parrying (you have to unlock parry, which I did not like, but okay, it’s one of the first upgrades). The first change is that your shield, a.k.a. your shell, is not a thing you equip permanently and forget about. The shells are interchangeable and they break (a lot), they have different stats, they have different, specific abilities you can use. You are not expected to keep one shell, and it is practically impossible: you will have to change, use the different abilities, defend yourself from the attacks with it, each time closer to being broken. Going without one is not advised since you receive a lot more damage, lose stats and lose some “perks” that you can equip but which are only active when you have a shell. Luckily, there are various ways to preserve your favorite shell (and believe me, you will have favorite shells, because they look fun, because of the stats or the abilities): you can learn how to parry like a pro (more on that later), you can prefer to dodge the abilities and shield less, or you can even buy insurance for your shell which gives you your favorite shell (for some good old microplastics) every time you get out of the bonfire. So if you like soulslike games, but the more lite, more approachable ones, this one should be for you.

The parry is one of my favorite mechanics, it makes you feel good, it makes you feel that you are outplaying the boss itself, that you are, for a moment, invincible. That’s why games like Nine Sols — a Taiwanese game with fighting and platforming that revolves around the parry —, Furi — a boss-rush game with heavy parry requirements — and (I only played a little, did not finish, I know, I should) Sekiro are games that I love and that also frustrate me a little, because I always want to parry, like an obsession. This game makes the parry a little different. Normally, parries need you to perform X action at the time the enemy attacks. Normally, you press the parry button, the protect/guard button, at the right moment. But this game makes the parry a thing that you undo: you parry when you un-shield yourself, when you stop pressing the guarding trigger, when Kril leaves the shell. It’s not a game-changer, but it brings novelty, brings something different, you have to pre-emptively shield yourself so you can parry, but then shielding makes you slower. So you have to be careful because there are attacks (blue ones) that are specialized in destroying your shield/guard, and red attacks that are unblockable. This all together changes the normal timing that you are used to in other games and changes your muscle memory a little. All this, together with the shell abilities that I have talked about and other “magic”, makes for a rich combat experience.

But the fight mechanics do not exist in a vacuum, the combat is as good as the mechanics together with the enemies, bosses, etc. If using your combat abilities and mechanics is frustrating against the enemies, it can be as fluid and good as you want but it will feel like shit; if the enemies and bosses are too easy, you will not feel that you are using them fully, or that you can experience the mechanics to their full extent. So I have to talk about them too. First, the enemies. Like you would suspect, most of them are crabs or sea creatures like some kind of fish or crustacean. There is some variety, not the most variety ever, but for a game of this size it is enough. I would say the types of enemies are different enough to fit the boxes you would expect a soulslike to have: the slow one that fucks you up in one hit, the fast one that dies faster but, because it is so fast, is annoying, the swarming one, the ones that inflict status effects and are annoying, the super-OP enemy that you think could be a miniboss sometimes, and the occasional enemy that, for some reason, one-shots you every time because it literally has a 1-hit-KO skill. Overall, the enemies connect well with the gameplay; the timings, the cadence, the position and density are well balanced, so you do not feel that it is too easy or that there are too many enemies attacking and you lose control of the situation. Of course there are some exceptions, some later stages have particularly annoying enemies that, personally, are not fun, and there is one area that is overloaded with ranged enemies which, combined with the fact that you do not have any way of attacking them besides parrying the projectiles, can be a little frustrating. The bosses are a bit underwhelming. As of the time of writing, there are three minor bosses that I have not beaten, because I did not go to find them and did not find them in my normal playthrough. There are good bosses, but, for my liking, most of them are easy; if you have the necessary abilities, the shells with good stats and know how to parry some of the attacks, you can beat them in a few tries. In terms of diversity, some of them feel samey, others feel like they tried to do something different and we were left with a boss that is not as fun as it should be. Again, the bosses are good, but I hoped for more, they have too little variety in attack types and combos for my taste. There was only one that was hard (the final one) and another that took me more time, because I felt that one of the attacks was very buggy.

This brings me to one of the aspects that I would like not to have to talk about. The bugs, bugs, bugs, bugs. There are bugs, not enough to make the experience unbearable, but enough to make me notice them and sometimes ruin some fights or sections of gameplay. From enemies and the character clipping into the floor, from being unable to do any action besides moving, characters/NPCs that get bugged and do not talk, jumps that do not go as intended, the clipping on enemies that makes you or them jump insanely high, and so on. Again, they happen, but they are not horrible. But they do fuck up some fights and some platforming.

The platforming… Exists. It’s not the main dish in this game and is there only to make the game more than a sequence of fights. There is the typical stuff: climbing some kind of meshes, jumping, gliding, hookshot and so on. Almost none of the platforming is challenging, and it should not be, given the intent of the game, but some parts were a bit janky. Continuing a bit from the bugs, the jumps from the meshes did not feel the best, the camera angles after using the hookshot sometimes point in a completely different direction from the next jump/hookshot. On top of that, some zones, as in a typical soulslike, seem designed to fuck you. While you try to do some parkour and some ranged enemies fuck you mid-air, you fall, you get damaged or die and start again. These last scenarios happened very few times, I just had to get it off my chest. Was it great platforming? No. Does the game have the best movement and fluid actions? Also no. Related to this, I think the running speed is a bit too slow for the size of some of the zones, and the shell roll, which can accelerate you, can be used very little and only downhill, which is kinda disappointing. But at least the scenarios that you visit are, in their own way, diverse. From the obligatory Miyazaki swamp that poisons you, to the Anor Londo / Farum Azula-like structure, passing through some kind of forest and deep caves with little lighting. I was impressed by how they (the developers) were capable of taking advantage of the rich diversity of the ocean to make different environments for the game. With the more cartoony graphics, we do not get awesome, realistic or impressive views, but they are pretty or interesting enough to make you stop a bit and look at them in the game. All this together with the fitting songs, which were good. With a notable mention for the first boss music, that, like a saw, sliced my eardrums and tingled my brain in the right way.

Going back to the journey of our little hermit crab, if you see it through until the end: you will be amused at some points with the puns, curious about the light Capitalism and meritocracy critiques, and interested in the — although predictable — magical, deity background that it takes on in the later half of the game. You can see the struggles of the side characters, their personalities. The journey of Kril, whose only objective is getting their shell back and who, for that, goes after a treasure to pay the pawn shop that has the shell. Which unveils a lot of other stuff that I will not say because of spoilers. The side cast is basically a spread of attitudes towards the trash economy. From Firth, the believer in his own intelligence, in the free market, in meritocracy, who sucks his own dick too much and is blind to the systemic oppression of the people who live in it. From the noble Chitan, who also does not understand everything that happens. From Nemma, the normal citizen, who shows and tells us the preoccupation of the normal people, of the poor. To the historian who wants to find the treasure that will help everyone, and Roland, the big capitalist, who seems to believe he is entitled to it. Together, from true believer to crushed, they are the message. The story is entertaining enough, but sometimes I think it takes itself a bit too seriously and maybe it would be better if it were more comedy-adjacent. Again, it is a good story, but it does not bring anything new to the table and I will probably forget it. The most interesting point is the evolution of Kril, from the shy crab that had to get out of their shore to the angry one who has had enough crab of the people, of the fighting, of the oppression.

In the end, the game takes between 13 and 17 hours to complete the main story plus some extras; the game is not expensive ($29.99) and it surely is worth it. Again, it will not be a novelty, but it will be fun, funny and a good time, and sometimes that is enough. Sometimes you just want to have your shell, sit on a shore and relax and not think about taxes.
