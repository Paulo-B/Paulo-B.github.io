Everdeep Aurora

Aug 26, 2026

6/10

Personal rating

Everdeep Aurora is a game. A great game? No. A bad game? Neither. Would I recommend it to a friend, a colleague, or a game aficionado? Probably. It wouldn’t be the first on the list, but I could. When I was deciding what rating to give, on the meaningless scale of 1-10, I could not decide. This is not a game that I hate, it’s not frustrating, it’s not badly made, and it’s not full of bugs or any of those problems. But at the same time, I could not tell you anything the game excels at.

The developers are Nautilus Games, a two-person indie studio from Madrid, Spain. I love indie games with small developer teams, normally, the greatest ideas, innovation, and so on are born from there. Games like Hollow Knight, In Stars and Time, Omori, and Look Outside are among my favorite games. But this one, sadly, is not. Let me start again from the beginning. The game starts with Shell, the main protagonist, waking up on a bench with a letter from her mom. She then starts her mission of searching for her mom. From there you start your adventure. You talk with an NPC who is a frog (Ribbert), who gives you a Drill, and you start exploring. From there your job is to dig and find the next thing. You find NPCs that give you things and quests, and you try to understand the world, what happened, and why everyone is underground, and so on. But that’s all you really do. The adventure part does not progress well; you get some upgrades that help with movement and feel nice, like a wall jump (used once), a glide, and a big propulsion jump. But the platforming is not very complex or engaging, the puzzles are easy, and most of the time I don’t know which quests I’m doing and which I’m not. I tried to understand the lore better, but I could not, as far as I know there’s a meteor shower ruining the world, and there are people who are devoted to some bots they created, having locked their sins deep underground, which they call a monster. In the end, I didn’t understand if the monster is literal, if the people on the other side simply died, or how any of it relates to the meteor shower. Maybe I skimmed the text? Or maybe they just couldn’t make me care. Going back to the gameplay, the controls are sometimes confusing, the menu is accessible at the same time you’re playing, and you use the arrow keys, but only left and right to move in the inventory, and up and down for something else. Also, for some reason the map is there but has almost nothing in it. The main buildings? Yes. The upgrades you get? Yes. Are they useful? Not much. Secret entrances you’ve already discovered, places you’ve already been? Nope, none of that is on the map. The one thing that was genuinely good in the game, unlike the rest of the aspects I’ve talked about, was the music. It was calming, fit the vibe of the game, and felt relaxing.

Overall, I was disappointed, not because the game was short (5 hours), but because it lacks depth. Which is kind of ironic for a game about excavation.
